What we're working on

One problem runs through most of this lab: disease of the hip abductors. It is a common cause of lateral hip pain, and the field has not settled what to call it, how to measure recovery from it, or when to operate. The program below works that problem end to end, on a platform of population-scale records, biomechanical models, and robotic case data that answers questions well beyond it.

Hip abductor disease

Greater trochanteric pain syndrome affects roughly one in six adults over 50, and the lesion behind it is usually a tendon rather than a bursa. Even so, the literature describes the same tear under competing names, no outcome instrument has been validated for these patients, and no payment model exists for treating them.

The same pathology appears under competing acronyms, with terminology borrowed from the rotator cuff and little cross-citation between the groups describing it. Our scoping review consolidates nine of those studies into one account of undersurface detachment of the hip abductor tendons: what the lesion is, how it presents, and how it has been repaired.

How common it is remains unsettled, because published incidence comes from single health networks and does not generalize. We are merging federated record networks to estimate that properly and to find what the disease travels with. One line of that work is a cohort of 385,868 women testing whether post-menopausal hormone therapy changes the risk of greater trochanteric pain syndrome, and whether the timing of initiation matters. Establishing the size of the problem is what makes the case for research funding, device development, and reimbursement.

Recovery is difficult to measure because almost none of the instruments in use were validated for these patients. We assessed the gluteal outcome instruments against COSMIN standards, and separately asked whether preoperative fatty infiltration on MRI predicts gait-specific rather than global function after repair.

Repairs fail at meaningful rates, and augmenting them with a scaffold is increasingly common without a clear mechanical rationale. Cadaveric testing and patient-specific finite element models let us measure how a construct shares load, where it concentrates stress, and which repairs are at risk.

Treatment spans physical therapy, image-guided injection, open repair, and endoscopic technique, and the evidence for each sits in a separate literature. We pooled them into a single stage-based pathway for tears confirmed on imaging. We also study the surgical approach itself, including a scoping review of the Superior Transverse Anatomic Reconstruction approach to hip replacement.

The end point is agreement. With radiology, primary care, and physical therapy we are building a consensus process covering how this disease is named on imaging, diagnosed in clinic, and managed after surgery.

The platform

The infrastructure the abductor work runs on also answers questions well beyond the hip.

Population-scale clinical data

Record networks hold the histories of hundreds of millions of patients, enough to run controlled analyses no trial could. The work is as methodological as it is clinical: confounding by indication, immortal-time and selection bias, and estimates that hold up across institutions.

Federated EHR analysis · Pharmacoepidemiology · Causal inference · Survival modeling

Computational biomechanics

Some questions are mechanical and no trial can answer them: how a construct shares load, where it concentrates stress, where it fails first. We build patient-specific models from clinical imaging and test them against cadaveric mechanics.

Finite element analysis · Patient-specific modeling · Cadaveric testing · Image-based meshing

Robotics and intraoperative data

Robotic platforms record every cut, gap, and alignment angle, and almost none of it is ever analyzed. We treat the robot's plan as a prediction and test it against what the surgeon actually finds. As native coronal deformity increases, the platform systematically underpredicts the medial gap, which changes how the knee should be balanced.

Robotic arthroplasty data · Prediction vs ground truth · Registry linkage

Value and cost of care

Payment models price risk by what care costs to deliver, not by what it is charged. We measure cost from the bottom up, by the time each resource is used across a full cycle of care, and compare that against what hospital accounting reports.

Time-driven activity-based costing · Micro-costing · Value-based care

AI agents and tooling

The lab runs on software it builds: agents that synthesize evidence, assemble cohorts from messy clinical data, and draft structured documentation. Because they operate where care happens, we hold them to a clinical standard, evaluated and audited against the task they claim to do.

LLM & agent engineering · Retrieval & evidence synthesis · FHIR / clinical data · Evaluation & safety

Where the work is headed

Predicting which abductor tendon repairs fail

Gluteus medius repairs fail at meaningful rates, and outcomes vary widely. Using imaging, intraoperative findings, and longitudinal outcomes from our repair cohort, the goal is a model that identifies repairs at high risk of failure and the factors that drive it.

Data: imaging, operative, outcomes  ·  Methods: ML, survival analysis

From intraoperative data to a structured operative record

Robotic platforms log detailed data for every case, but little of it reaches surgical registries because the translation is manual. The problem is an agentic pipeline that converts intraoperative and EHR data into structured, auditable documentation a surgeon would sign.

Data: robotic logs, EHR  ·  Methods: LLM agents, FHIR

Separating cause from association in drug-osteoarthritis screens

A medication-wide screen surfaces many drugs associated with osteoarthritis. Association is not cause. The work is to take the strongest signals and test them with negative controls, target-trial emulation, and sensitivity analysis to see what holds.

Data: federated EHR  ·  Methods: causal inference, pharmacoepidemiology

A fast surrogate for patient-specific finite element analysis

A full finite element model of a repair construct takes hours to build and solve. A learned surrogate that predicts the stress field from imaging in seconds, accurately enough to inform a decision, would make this usable in clinic.

Data: imaging, FE models  ·  Methods: surrogate modeling, geometry processing

Correcting the robot's gap prediction across deformity

Robotic knee replacement underpredicts the medial gap as native coronal deformity increases. The problem is to characterize that error across the full deformity range and build a correction a surgeon can use intraoperatively.

Data: robotic arthroplasty  ·  Methods: modeling, data science

A reproducible classification for concealed abductor tears

Undersurface abductor tears sit beneath intact superficial fibers and are described in the literature under competing names. The work is an imaging-based, reproducible classification, ideally with automated segmentation, that the field can use consistently.

Data: MRI  ·  Methods: segmentation, clinical research
